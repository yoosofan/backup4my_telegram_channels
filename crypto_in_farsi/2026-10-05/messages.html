To claim coins on both sides of a blockchain split, your safest option is to use a non-custodial wallet where you hold your own private keys, such as [Trezor](https://trezor.io) or [Nunchuk](https://bip110.org). This allows you to split the coins manually by exporting your private keys or using a transaction tool.

If a contentious chain split occurs, follow these steps to secure both coins:

1. Do Not Transact Immediately: Wait until the split occurs and both networks stabilize. Moving funds before splitting increases the risk of "replay attacks" (where a transaction broadcasted on one network is copied and broadcasted on the other).
2. Transfer to a Non-Custodial Wallet: Move your original funds off exchanges and into a wallet like Trezor or Electrum that supports comprehensive coin control.
3. Wait for Split Tools: Developers usually release specific "split tools" or guides (e.g., via [Electron Cash](https://bitcoin.com) in past splits) that let you move one coin without moving the other.
4. Use Coin Control: Use a wallet that allows you to manually isolate and manage specific UTXOs (unspent transaction outputs) so you can safely send your coins on one chain while keeping the other intact.

For the most secure and localized handling during a fork, verify updates on platform forums and utilize established wallet applications like [Trezor](https://trezor.io).
